Zoho Analytics vs Power BI: Licences, Data Sources and Fit for Zoho and Tally Users

Zoho Analytics and Power BI can both turn sales, stock and finance data into dashboards. The real differences are in how you pay for viewers, how data reaches the tool, and which suite the rest of your business already runs on. For many Indian manufacturers there is one more question: how to get TallyPrime data in.

Quick answer

Choose Zoho Analytics if your data lives mostly in Zoho apps or TallyPrime and many people need to view dashboards, since its plans bundle users and rows. Choose Power BI if you run on Microsoft 365 and your analysts work in Excel and Power BI Desktop. With Power BI Pro, people who view shared reports also need a paid licence unless the content sits on Fabric capacity of F64 or larger.

Most comparisons of Zoho Analytics and Power BI end with “Power BI for Microsoft shops, Zoho for everyone else”. That is a fair starting point, but it skips the two things that decide the bill and the workload: who needs a paid licence just to look at a report, and how your data gets into the tool every day.

Every plan detail below was checked on Zoho’s and Microsoft’s own pages on 23 October 2026. We do not quote prices, because both change; see the official Zoho Analytics pricing and Power BI pricing pages. We build on Zoho, so read this with that in mind; we say where Power BI is the better fit.

The short answer

  • Choose Zoho Analytics if your data lives mostly in Zoho apps or TallyPrime, you want a plan that bundles users and data rows, and you want to share dashboards with many people without a paid seat for each viewer.
  • Choose Power BI if your business runs on Microsoft 365, your analysts already work in Excel and the Power BI Desktop app, and your data sits in SQL Server, SharePoint or Azure.
  • Count viewers before you compare. With Power BI Pro, anyone who opens a shared report needs a paid licence too, unless the content sits on Fabric capacity of F64 or larger. That one rule changes the comparison more than any feature.

Two ways to license the same dashboard

Zoho Analytics sells plans that bundle a number of users and data rows. Its pricing page lists Free, Basic, Standard, Premium and Enterprise, plus a Dedicated Compute option for very large data. The Free plan covers 2 users and 10,000 rows. Paid plans start at 2 users and 0.5 million rows on Basic, 5 users and 1 million rows on Standard, 15 users and 5 million rows on Premium, and 50 users and 50 million rows on Enterprise. You can add users, rows and viewers as add-ons, and pay monthly or yearly. Paid plans have a 15-day trial.

Power BI is licensed per user. The Free licence lets one person build reports, but Microsoft’s pricing page says you must upgrade to Pro or Premium to share them. Pro lets you publish and share, and Microsoft notes that people consuming that content need a paid per-user licence as well. Premium Per User raises the limits. Fabric capacity is bought for the organisation; on F64 or larger, Microsoft says content consumers can view and interact without their own paid licence, while authors still need Pro. Power BI Pro is also included in Microsoft 365 E5 and Office 365 E5.

So a dashboard for a production manager, a plant head and ten supervisors means twelve paid seats on Power BI Pro. On Zoho Analytics those twelve count against the plan’s users or are bought as viewers.

Getting your data in

Zoho apps. Zoho Analytics has its own connectors for Zoho CRM, Zoho Books, Zoho Inventory and other Zoho apps, each with ready-made reports. The sync frequency depends on the plan: once a day on Basic, up to 8 times on Standard and Premium, and up to 24 on Enterprise. Basic is limited to any two business app connectors; Standard and above include 50+. If your CRM, accounts and stock are already in Zoho, this is the shortest path to a dashboard.

Microsoft data. Power BI is at home with Excel, SharePoint, SQL Server and Azure. Pro refreshes a dataset up to 8 times a day, and Premium Per User and capacity up to 48. If your figures already live in Microsoft systems, Power BI is the shorter path.

Data inside your network. Both tools need a small program on a local machine to reach a database on your own server. Zoho’s is Zoho Databridge, which runs on Windows, macOS and Linux and pulls data from databases such as MySQL, SQL Server, Oracle and PostgreSQL on a schedule. Microsoft’s is the on-premises data gateway, a Windows application that connects local sources to Power BI and other Microsoft cloud services. It comes in a standard mode that many users share and a personal mode for one user.

TallyPrime data, for Indian businesses

Many Indian manufacturers and traders keep their accounts in TallyPrime even after they move sales or stock to the cloud. Zoho Analytics has a documented TallyPrime connector built on Zoho Databridge. You enable ODBC in TallyPrime, install Databridge (version 10.9.8 or later) on the Tally machine or a local server that can reach it, and choose what to sync: ledgers, godowns, stock items, vouchers and the trial balance, plus reports such as the balance sheet, profit and loss, stock summary and the sales and purchase registers. Data syncs every 3, 6 or 12 hours or daily, and every hour on Enterprise.

We could not find a Microsoft-built TallyPrime connector for Power BI. Teams that use the two together go through Tally’s ODBC interface, scheduled exports, or a third-party connector sold separately, refreshed through the gateway. That works, but someone has to build, pay for and look after it. If you are still deciding whether Tally stays, our Zoho Books vs TallyPrime guide covers that first.

Building reports: browser or desktop

Zoho Analytics is built and used in the browser, with native mobile apps. A sales manager can drag fields into a chart or ask a question of the data in plain English with Ask Zia.

Power BI reports are mostly built in Power BI Desktop, a free Windows application (Windows 10 or Windows Server 2016 or later, according to Microsoft’s download page), and then published to the Power BI service. Desktop is where Power BI is strongest: its modelling and DAX formulas give an analyst fine control over complex calculations. So report authors need a Windows machine.

AI features

Zoho lists Ask Zia, automated and diagnostic insights, predictive analytics, what-if analysis and anomaly detection on its pricing page, and offers an MCP server so AI agents and assistants can work with Zoho Analytics data.

Microsoft’s pricing page lists advanced AI with Premium Per User and capacity, and Copilot in Fabric with capacity. Check which licence each AI feature needs before you count on it.

On either tool, define your key measures once and check AI answers against a trusted report; our guide to data foundations in Zoho Analytics shows how.

What a manufacturer should test

For a factory, the useful first dashboards are usually plain: orders due this week against production, stock below reorder level, rejection and rework by product, and receivables by customer. Our guide to manufacturing dashboards and KPIs lists what to measure first. In either trial, build one of these on your own data and check three things: how fresh the figures are when the shift supervisor opens it, how many people need a licence to see it, and who will fix it when a source changes.

A trial plan for both

  • Count the people who will build reports and the people who will only view them.
  • List every data source, and mark which ones sit on your own server.
  • Connect your two most important sources and note how often each refreshes on the plan you would buy.
  • Rebuild one report you use every week, and have the person who reads it check the numbers.
  • Share it with a viewer who has no licence yet, and see what they need.
